# Handoff: the fly crew to the Mun (2026-09-11, 08:00–13:20 session)

For whoever picks this up next, human or agent, without this chat. Read
[ksp.md](ksp.md#fly-me-to-the-mun) first; this file is the delta and the open threads.

## The project in one paragraph

Fly a KSP 1.12.5 rocket through kRPC with fruit-fly connectomes in the seats: Jeb on
pitch, Bill on yaw, Bob on the throttle (`flybywire/ksp/crew.py`), and a flight computer
(`flybywire/ksp/mun.py`, `MunMission`) that plans burns, works action groups, and hands
each seat an error signal. The task of this phase was **"Fly me to the Mun"**: launch from
the pad, circularize, trans-Munar injection onto a free-return trajectory, corrections,
flyby, return, entry and splashdown, with every attitude and throttle command passing
through a connectome. The autopilot crew (plain PID seats) proved the plan first
(`runs/mun-autopilot-8`); this session made the fly crew do it, repeatedly, and hardened
the planner against the ways it found to die.

## What was wrong and what changed (in order)

1. **Attitude loop.** From `mun-flies-1` telemetry: the fly is a 0.13 stick/deg proportional
   controller with a 0.32 s lag saturating near 5°; the upper stage gives 21–31 deg/s² per
   unit stick on wheels. At `authority 0.7, damping 0.03` that is ζ≈0.2 with 25° phase loss,
   a ±8° limit cycle (simulated and matched). **Fix**: per-crew augmentation,
   `CREW_AUGMENTATION` in `flybywire/ksp/mun.py`: flies `0.5 / 0.12`, autopilot unchanged
   `0.7 / 0.03`. `--authority/--damping` still override.
2. **Gimbal chatter.** Under thrust the Poodle gives 62–77 deg/s² per unit stick (rises as the
   tanks empty); the wheel-tuned rate loop hit the stops at 1 Hz, ±3°, whenever the engine
   was lit (ascent on the Poodle and every burn). **Fix**: `MunConfig.thrust_authority`
   (default 2.0, `--thrust-authority`): on the upper stage (`flags["upper"]`, set at booster
   separation and whenever the phase leaves ascent) authority and damping are divided by
   `1 + thrust_authority × last_throttle`. Booster exempt. Result: TMI in mission 4 held
   0.6° mean, max 1.5°, zero interlock events, zero saturated ticks.
3. **Overshoot backstop** now judged on velocity-to-be-gained along the fixed burn attitude
   (`along`), not the remaining-Δv magnitude, which grows with lateral wander.
4. **Graceful aborts**: falling back into the atmosphere with the mission ahead, and a
   maneuver node KSP removed under us, both end with an `abort` event and a written
   `summary.json` instead of a traceback.
5. **Milestone quicksaves and resume**: `--save-milestones` writes `flybywire-orbit` on
   entering `plan_tmi` and `flybywire-tmi` on first entering `coast_to_mun` (once each).
   `--quicksave flybywire-orbit` (or `-tmi`) resumes: `to_pad` accepts an orbiting vessel,
   `resume_in_space` picks the phase from the orbit (low Kerbin orbit → `plan_tmi`; out at the
   Mun's distance → `coast_to_mun`, which handles a missing encounter; in the Mun's SOI →
   `mun_flyby`; high with periapsis in the air → `return_coast`). The load waits until the
   game clock has moved by more than real time can explain, because the vessel being left
   behind can itself look "ready".
6. **Lost / late encounter.** A fly TMI leaves ~10–14 m/s of lateral residual; the corridor
   is 2 m/s. Both fly TMIs missed the first pass and (the orbit is near a 2:1 resonance with
   the Mun) picked up a very sensitive second-revolution encounter (80,000 s out; a 2 m/s
   correction residual lost it again in mission 4). **Fix** (committed): `coast_to_mun` treats
   "no encounter within one orbital period" as something to correct immediately:
   `correction_node(first_pass=True, span=40, max_dv=150)` searches a ±40 m/s prograde/radial
   grid then a 3-axis (prograde, radial, normal) pattern search, scoring only encounters inside
   one period. Up to two tries; if none is in reach it logs `later_pass` and flies the later
   encounter as before. The generic pattern search also takes a `score` callable now. All
   corrections are now three-axis (mission 5's was 14 m/s radial + 0.6 normal). **Status**: in
   mission 5 the fly's TMI happened to keep a first-pass encounter, so the `lost_encounter`
   branch has been exercised only in its earlier two-axis form (mission 4, second-pass) and
   the `first_pass=True` scoring has not yet been hit in flight (mission 6's encounter counted
   as first pass, t_soi 53,000 s inside a 66,000 s period).
7. **Mun impact is now fatal in the score** (after mission 6). `trajectory_score` returns
   `1000 + (15_000 − mun)/100` for any Mun periapsis below 15 km, graded so pattern search can
   climb out, and always worse than 400 (no return). `solve_free_return` raises (→ mission
   abort) if its best solution still hits the Mun. `mun_flyby` gained a one-time emergency
   correction: on entering the Mun's sphere with periapsis < 20 km and > 600 s to go, it
   searches a 3-axis node (`max_dv=200`, `span=40`) and burns it. The scoring change is
   proven by mission 7 (189 km plan, 166 km flyby, home); the emergency correction has been
   written but never fired in flight, so treat it as untested.
8. **Blind coast toward a dropped encounter** (after the fly-9 stall). `coast_to_mun`
   records `flags["soi_expected"]` whenever an encounter is visible; if the later pass has
   been accepted and the conics show no encounter, it warps half the remaining time
   (`blind_warp` event) and looks again, and aborts if the expected time passes by 600 s
   with nothing in sight. Validated on fly-9's second attempt: `blind_warp remaining=53271`,
   encounter back 26,600 s later, normal warps and midcourse look resumed.
9. **Provoking the flyby emergency, what did not work.** Attempt 1 (from orbit): the TMI
   solver with a 10 km target and no floor still planned 125 km, because the return term
   dominates; the midcourse would have pulled it down but the encounter went missing
   (item 8) and my manual warp from a side client overran the node it then planned
   ("burn never progressed"). Attempt 2 (from the TMI save): the midcourse look came at
   t_soi 11,656 s and moving the periapsis from 327 km to 10 km there cost 75 m/s, over
   the 60 m/s `max_dv`, so `no_correction "too expensive"` and a plain flyby. A third
   attempt was ready but not flown: `/tmp/provoke_flyby.py` (reproduced below) sets
   `later_pass_accepted=True` and `t_soi0=1e9` in `__init__` so the midcourse fires on the
   first tick after the TMI save loads, when 300 km of periapsis costs a few m/s.

## Open threads, in priority order

1. **Max-Q yaw departure on every ascent.** In missions 4, 6 and 8 the yaw error leaves
   at 55 s, peaks at −15° / −18° / −22° at 65–75 s (8–12 km, same sign every time) with
   Bill's stick hard over at −0.5…−0.8 the whole time, and comes back by 90 s in 4 and 6.
   In `runs/mun-flies-8-crashed` it held −20° for 15 s longer, the velocity vector tipped
   over, and the thick-air rule (`max_aoa_deg` 3° of surface prograde below `aoa_altitude`)
   made the program follow prograde down: at 79 s the program wanted 48° pitch, prograde
   was at 25°, the nose went with prograde and the ship hit the sea at 240 s under full
   throttle with pitch error near zero. Not a fly problem (the stick was right and steady)
   and not today's code (the ascent is untouched since mission 4). Two things to do:
   (a) find the cause: same sign, same second in three runs is a systematic asymmetry, so
   look at the yaw target frame in `truth()` during the pitch program (roll rate builds
   from 0 to 0.37°/s over the same window) or an aerodynamic asymmetry of the stack, by
   plotting `yaw_error_deg`, `roll_rate` and `controls.yaw` for 40–100 s of every
   `mission.jsonl`; (b) make the thick-air AoA rule asymmetric so a falling prograde can
   never drag the commanded pitch below the program by more than the AoA limit; a nose a
   few degrees above prograde turns prograde back up, a nose that follows it does not.
   Also worth a 0.7 yaw authority in the atmosphere only.
2. **A 46 km entry skips.** Fly-9b came home at 46.3 km periapsis because the return
   correction's burn was off by 6 km (lateral error; the computer backstop ended it) and
   the trim that would have fixed it (34.9 m/s) was refused by the trim cost cap
   (`no_correction "too expensive"`). The capsule dipped to 46.9 km, lost only enough
   energy to fall from a 6,000 km apoapsis to 800 km, and went back up with 8% battery.
   Mission 8 at 43 km trimmed 13 m/s and was fine; 40 km is the target for a reason. Two
   fixes, both cheap: never refuse a return trim on cost when the periapsis is above
   ~42 km (a skip costs the crew, a burn costs fuel the stage has: 430 monoprop, the
   Poodle's tanks), and after `trimmed_back` re-check once more at ~200 km altitude so a
   bad trim burn gets a second one. Also worth knowing: the entry phase has no idea it
   skipped; `power_low` at 128,592 then stood the attitude seats down.
3. **Flyby altitude floor** is now `MunConfig.mun_floor` = 30 km, shared by the score, the
   TMI refusal and the flyby emergency. It is still a cliff: above it the return term
   outweighs the Mun term 10:1. Fine for the solver (prefers 160–300 km), watch corrections.
4. **The first-pass recovery fired in mission 8 and found nothing.** After a TMI cut 12 m/s
   short by Bob, `midcourse_check look=lost_encounter first_pass=false t_soi=80600`, then
   `no_correction ... score_before 1e6 score_after 1e6 first_pass=true`: every candidate in
   the 3-axis search scored "no encounter at all", including the +12 m/s prograde point that
   should have put the first pass back. Most likely the `first_pass` filter in
   `correction_node.score_of` is nulling everything: it compares the node orbit's
   `time_to_soi_change` against `self.v.orbit.period`, and right after TMI the live conics
   report NaN for that (the same flakiness item 11b in ksp.md describes) or the ellipse's
   period is shorter than the first-pass encounter time. Reproduce from mission 8's
   `flybywire-tmi` save with `--stop-after coast_to_mun` and print `t_soi`, `period`
   for a few nodes by hand. Until then the fallback works: the mission accepted the
   second-pass encounter (270 km, return 39 km) and flew it. The better fix remains
   constraining `solve_free_return` itself to first-pass solutions.
5. **Return leg after a low flyby** exits at −69 km (mission 4) / −73 km (autopilot 8) and needs
   a ~110–230 m/s correction; a first-pass flyby at 53 km exited at 46 km and needed 26.
   Flybys at 166 km (mission 7) and 300 km (autopilot 8's plan) exit inside the corridor.
6. **Entry controller thrash** under chutes: dozens of `turn_around` events and sticks on the
   stops below 10 km. Harmless, noisy. Stand the attitude seats down once the drogue is out.
7. **Event log ordering**: fixed (`load` is logged once the save is up, `node` events carry
   the tick's UT and the node's as `node_ut`). Older runs' `events.jsonl` still read out of order.
8. **Panels** `panel-*.png` are the last 90×160 retina frames (black throttle panel at

## Operational lessons (do not repeat)

- **One flight computer per ship.** Sending SIGINT to the shell wrapper of a backgrounded
  `uv run flybywire mun ...` does not stop Python. Mission 3's process kept running while
  `mun-flies-3b` started; both drove the same vessel (warp fights, "burn never progressed").
  Before starting any run: `ps aux | rg "flybywire mun"` and kill the *python* PID. `runs/
  mun-flies-3b` is that contaminated run; ignore it (left in place, not deleted).
- **A killed process's `warp_to` keeps running inside KSP.** kRPC executes `warp_to` server-side
  until the target UT; killing the client leaves a ghost in `conn.krpc.clients` (name
  `flybywire-mun`) and the game keeps warping, even across a quickload (mission 7's first
  attempt loaded `flybywire-orbit` at ut 20,920 and the game warped straight on toward
  mission 6's target of 77,803, while the new process hung in `sc.load`). Setting
  `rails_warp_factor = 0` or issuing a new `warp_to` does not cancel it. Cure: wait until UT
  passes the ghost's target (mission events log `warp ... "to"`), or restart the kRPC server
  from its in-game window, then load the save again. Better still, do not kill a mission
  while it is warping; SIGINT it during a coast tick.
- **Never `--stop-after` and then resume without `--save-milestones`**; the resume needs the
  save. `flybywire-orbit` and `flybywire-tmi` are overwritten by each run that writes them.
- KSP's conics are inconsistent between a live orbit and the same state reloaded (the live
  game showed no encounter after mission 3's TMI; the reloaded save showed the second-pass
  one). Trust `time_to_soi_change` only as far as one period.
- `quicksave #1` is the crewed stack on the pad; kRPC server on 127.0.0.1:50000/50001; KSP
  must be in the flight scene. `uv run flybywire ksp --check` confirms.

## Follow-up: max-Q ascent fix (same day, next worker)

Focused on open thread 1. The current changes are in `flybywire/ksp/mun.py`,
`flybywire/cli.py`, `tests/test_mun.py` and `docs/ksp.md` item 15.

- The yaw target transform is correct. With the stack rolled about 90° and heading
  east, the gravity turn is physically on Bill's vessel yaw axis. The repeated
  negative yaw error is the nose falling ahead of the turn, not a 20° heading error.
  The diagnostic readings support insufficient control authority against aerodynamic
  loading; they do not rule out stack asymmetry.
- Default atmospheric ascent authority is now 0.7 on both vessel axes, returning to
  the existing fly value 0.5 above 45 km or when ascent ends. Damping and thrust
  scaling stay as before. `--ascent-authority` overrides this separately; an explicit
  `--authority` still sets both unless the ascent override is also supplied.
- The prograde clamp now refuses a command farther from vertical than `program + cap`.
  It retains the usual 3°/10° limits for the normal gravity turn, but recovery can
  exceed the AoA cap. The recorded failed fly-8 state at 16.2 km now commands 55.0°
  from vertical instead of 80.5°. That recovery bound is regression-tested; the
  strengthened ascents did not reproduce the failed trajectory to test it live.
- Logs now include commanded pitch, prograde heading, vessel heading/roll, dynamic
  pressure, aerodynamic pitch/sideslip and ascent authority.
